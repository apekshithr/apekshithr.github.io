Every product starts simple. Then it meets the world, and the world asks for more: one more setting, one more button, one more exception. Each request is reasonable on its own. Together, they bury the thing you set out to make.

Simplicity is not the absence of clutter. It is the result of understanding a problem so deeply that you know what actually matters -- and having the conviction to remove everything else.

> Simplicity is not a style. It is a consequence of clarity.

## The cost of addition

Adding a feature feels like progress. You can point at it, demo it, put it on a slide. Removing one rarely gets the same applause, yet it is often the more valuable move.

A few questions I keep returning to:

- Who is this *really* for, and what are they trying to do?
- If we removed this entirely, who would notice?
- Does this make the common case easier, or just the rare case possible?

## Care you can feel

People rarely notice good design directly. They notice that something feels calm, obvious, trustworthy. That feeling is the sum of a thousand small decisions nobody will ever see -- the spacing, the timing of an animation, the words on a button.

That care is the work. Everything else is decoration.
